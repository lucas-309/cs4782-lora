\section*{1. Introduction}

Fine-tuning a 175B-parameter language model for every downstream task is
prohibitive: a fresh full copy of the model per task costs as much as the
original training run and produces a 350\,GB checkpoint. \textbf{LoRA: Low-Rank
Adaptation of Large Language Models} (Hu et al., 2021)\,\footnote{\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685}{arXiv:2106.09685}}
sidesteps this by freezing the pre-trained weights and adding a trainable
\emph{rank-$r$} decomposition $\Delta W = BA$ in parallel to selected weight
matrices. The forward pass becomes $h = W_0 x + (\alpha/r) BA\, x$, with $A
\in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$. The paper's
central empirical claim is that this update has very low intrinsic rank ---
even $r{=}1$ or $r{=}2$ is enough to match full fine-tuning on many tasks.

\section*{2. Chosen result}

We reproduce a slice of \textbf{Table 2} of Hu et al.\ (2021): \textbf{RoBERTa-base
on GLUE}, comparing LoRA against full fine-tuning. Specifically we target
the \textbf{MRPC} cell (RoBERTa$_\text{base}$ + LoRA: 87.5; FT: 90.2) as
the headline result, and we additionally rerun the \textbf{rank ablation}
of Table 6 ($r \in \{1,2,4,8\}$) on RoBERTa-base / MRPC as our independent
experiment. We chose this slice because (i) it tests the central claim of
the paper at a scale a single Apple M-series GPU can handle in $\sim$5\,min
per run, and (ii) it lets us evaluate \emph{both} the parameter-efficiency
claim (LoRA matches FT with a tiny fraction of the params) and the
intrinsic-rank claim (rank doesn't matter much) with the same backbone.

\section*{3. Methodology}

\textbf{Implementation.} We wrote LoRA from scratch in $\sim$60 lines
(\texttt{code/lora.py}): a \texttt{LoRALinear} module wraps an
\texttt{nn.Linear}, adds trainable \texttt{lora\_A} (Kaiming-init) and
\texttt{lora\_B} (zero-init), and freezes the base linear. A helper walks
the model and replaces every attention \texttt{query} and \texttt{value}
projection with a \texttt{LoRALinear} of the chosen rank --- following
Table 5's recommendation that $\{W_q, W_v\}$ is the best two-target
configuration under a fixed parameter budget. We mark only LoRA parameters
and the new classification head as trainable. We deliberately avoid
\texttt{peft} so we know exactly what is being trained.

\textbf{Datasets.} GLUE MRPC (3.7k paraphrase pairs, accuracy) and SST-2
(67k sentiment sentences, accuracy), via \texttt{datasets.load\_dataset}.

\textbf{Training.} AdamW (weight decay 0.01), linear warmup over 6\% of
steps then linear decay. LoRA at $\text{lr}{=}5\!\times\!10^{-4}$,
full fine-tuning at $\text{lr}{=}2\!\times\!10^{-5}$. Batch size 32, max
sequence length 128, seed 42. MRPC: 3 epochs (115 steps each); SST-2: 2000
steps ($\sim$1 epoch) due to wall-clock budget. Hardware: one Apple M-series
GPU via PyTorch MPS.

\textbf{Modifications.} (a) RoBERTa-base instead of the paper's headline
GPT-3 175B (the paper still reports a RoBERTa-base cell, so we reproduce
that). (b) We add a \emph{head-only} baseline (frozen body, train only the
classifier) to verify that LoRA's lift is genuine. (c) The rank ablation
is on RoBERTa-base / MRPC rather than GPT-3 / WikiSQL.

\section*{4. Results \& analysis}

\textbf{Headline (MRPC).} Full FT 88.2\%; LoRA $r{=}8$ 86.3\%; LoRA $r{=}1$
86.0\%; head-only 68.4\%. Our LoRA number is $\sim$1.2 points below the
paper's reported RoBERTa$_\text{base}$ + LoRA (87.5\%) --- plausibly because
we use a single seed and fixed LR (the paper sweeps), and start from
RoBERTa-base instead of the MNLI-warm-started checkpoint that some prior
GLUE work uses for MRPC. The 18-point gap between LoRA and head-only is
the load-bearing observation: LoRA's lift is from the low-rank adaptation,
not from bookkeeping in the new classifier head.

\textbf{Rank ablation (MRPC).}
\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
$r$ & 1 & 2 & 4 & 8 \\
Trainable params & 629K & 666K & 739K & 887K \\
MRPC accuracy & 0.860 & 0.858 & 0.865 & 0.863 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}
The spread across ranks is 0.7 points, well within seed-to-seed noise on
the 408-example MRPC validation set ($\sim$0.24 points per misclassified
example). This reproduces the qualitative finding of Table 6 of the paper:
increasing $r$ does not unlock new directions in the update, suggesting
$\Delta W$ has a very low intrinsic rank.

\textbf{Second task (SST-2).} LoRA $r{=}8$ reaches \textbf{93.1\%} in a
$\sim$1-epoch budget; paper reports 95.1\% at the full sweep, a 2-point gap
that we expect closes with more steps.

\textbf{Discrepancies / challenges.} (i) \emph{The new classifier head
dominates the trainable count} at 125M scale: at $r{=}8$, $\sim$295K params
are LoRA and $\sim$592K are the head. The paper's ``10{,}000$\times$
headline'' is for GPT-3 175B where the head is negligible --- we frame the
poster accordingly. (ii) MRPC's 408-example validation set has high
seed-to-seed variance; with more compute we would average over 3+ seeds.
(iii) Apple MPS throughput is $\sim 1/3$--$1/4$ of a V100, which set the
ceiling on what we could sweep.

\textbf{In context.} The reproduction supports the paper's core argument:
fine-tuning is overparametrized, and a tiny additive low-rank update suffices
to adapt a pretrained Transformer. That this holds at 125M --- not just at
175B --- is encouraging for practitioners on modest hardware.

\section*{5. Reflections}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{From-scratch was the right call.} LoRA's machinery is small
enough that re-implementing it forced us to internalize what's frozen,
what's trained, and how the update scales with $r$ and $\alpha$. Calling
\texttt{peft.LoraConfig(\dots)} would have left that opaque.
\item \textbf{The crucial hyperparameter wasn't $r$ --- it was the LR.}
LoRA needs roughly $25\times$ the LR of full fine-tuning ($5e{-}4$ vs.\ $2e{-}5$)
because the matrices are tiny and zero-initialized; runs at $2e{-}5$ stayed
near random.
\item \textbf{Future directions.} (a) LoRA on MLP layers (the paper defers
this); (b) the subspace-overlap test from \S7.2 --- do different seeds
learn the same low-rank subspace? (c) full-rank fine-tuning on \emph{just}
$W_q$ and $W_v$, to separate ``low rank'' from ``few parameters.''
\item \textbf{With more compute:} 3 seeds per cell, LR sweep per rank, and
DeBERTa-XXL on one task --- the paper's DeBERTa numbers are its sharpest
match to full FT and would make for a more striking headline.
\end{itemize}
